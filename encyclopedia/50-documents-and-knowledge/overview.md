# Documents and Knowledge: Overview

Knowledge work generates mountains of documents: contracts, meeting notes, project documentation, client briefs, research reports. Without automation, valuable information gets buried in email threads and shared drives, never to be found again. This section contains approximately 26 automation patterns for document processing, knowledge management, and meeting documentation.

## Common Pain Points

**Pain Point 1: Can't Find Documents When Needed**
"I know we discussed that in a meeting somewhere..." Hours wasted searching.
*Automations that help*: Auto-tagging, smart filing, searchable transcripts.

**Pain Point 2: Manual Meeting Documentation**
Someone has to take notes, write summaries, extract action items, send to attendees.
*Automations that help*: Auto-transcription, AI summarization, action item extraction.

**Pain Point 3: Stale Knowledge Bases**
Documentation is outdated, questions go unanswered, tribal knowledge stays in people's heads.
*Automations that help*: Automated KB updates, FAQ generation, knowledge gaps detection.

## Scope

- **Document Intake and Tagging** (10+ automations): Capture, categorize, file, extract data
- **Knowledge Base Updates** (8-10 automations): Content creation, maintenance, search optimization
- **Meeting Notes and Summaries** (8-10 automations): Transcription, summarization, distribution, action tracking

## Tool Landscape

- **Document storage**: Google Drive, Dropbox, SharePoint, Box
- **Knowledge bases**: Notion, Confluence, GitBook, help desk platforms
- **Meeting tools**: Zoom, Teams, Google Meet
- **Transcription**: Otter.ai, Fireflies, Rev, Assembly AI
- **Note-taking**: Notion, Evernote, Obsidian, Roam

## Measuring Success

- **Time savings**: Hours spent documenting, searching, updating
- **Findability**: Time to find specific information (before vs after)
- **Knowledge coverage**: % of common questions documented
- **Action tracking**: Meeting action items completed vs dropped
